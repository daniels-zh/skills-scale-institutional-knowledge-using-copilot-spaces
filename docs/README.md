# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder contains the core project management and delivery guidance for OctoAcme. Together, these documents define how the team validates ideas, plans work, executes delivery, communicates status, manages risks, and improves the process over time.

## Project management process summary

OctoAcme follows a lightweight, repeatable lifecycle from initiative through improvement. During initiation, the team validates the business need, aligns stakeholders, defines success metrics, and decides whether a project should proceed. Planning then breaks work into a backlog, identifies dependencies, estimates effort, and sets milestones and a Definition of Done. In execution, the team uses daily standups, sprint or milestone reviews, and visible tracking to keep delivery moving while escalating blockers through the proper channels.

The process also emphasizes clear ownership and communication. Product managers define outcomes and prioritize the backlog, project managers coordinate schedules, risks, and stakeholder updates, and developers build and validate features against acceptance criteria. The team uses recurring syncs, demos, stakeholder updates, and structured escalation paths to keep work aligned and prevent unresolved issues from stalling progress. Risk management is handled through a shared risk register, ongoing monitoring, and communication templates that help the team surface issues early and escalate appropriately.

Quality assurance is embedded throughout the flow rather than left to the end. The team expects pull requests to include issue links and acceptance criteria, requires CI checks such as tests and linting, and uses unit, integration, smoke, and security validations before release. Deployment guidance includes staging checks, production verification, rollback considerations, and release notes so the team can ship with lower risk and clear operational awareness. After each sprint or major milestone, the group conducts retrospectives to capture wins, identify improvement opportunities, and assign follow-up actions.
